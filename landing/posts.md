These lead with insight/discussion, not a sales pitch — that's what survives community
self-promo rules and actually gets upvotes. **Always read the subreddit/forum rules first.**
Many communities want you to share the link only in a comment when someone asks, or in a
designated promo thread. When in doubt, post the discussion version and put the link in a
reply.

## A. r/dividends / r/investing — discussion-first (safest)

**Title:** Does anyone actually factor the tax bill into *when* they trim a position?

**Body:**
I've been managing my own portfolio for years and noticed something: every tracker I use
(Sharesight, Snowball, my brokerage) is great at showing what I *own*, but none of them help
with the decision that actually costs me money — when to sell.

Two mistakes I keep catching myself almost making:
1. Selling a winner a few weeks before it crosses the 1-year mark and getting taxed at the
   short-term rate for no reason.
2. Holding a clearly weakening position because I'm fuzzy on what the tax hit would be if I trimmed.

So I started building a tool for myself that scores each holding on quality (growth, margins,
debt, valuation vs. analyst targets) and then layers on the tax math — short-term vs. long-term
gain, the cost of selling today, and "X days until this turns long-term."

Question for the room: **how do you currently make trim decisions?** Gut feel, a spreadsheet,
or do you just not optimize for taxes at all? Trying to figure out if I'm the only one who finds
this annoying.

*(If folks are interested I'm happy to share what I've built — it's free while in testing.)*

## B. Bogleheads.org forum — measured, tax-savvy crowd

**Subject:** Tool idea: combining holding-quality flags with capital-gains timing — useful or overkill?

**Body:**
This forum is the most tax-aware community I know, so I'd value a reality check.

I'm building a personal tool that, for each holding, combines two things most trackers keep
separate: (1) a quality/valuation read on the position, and (2) the tax consequence of trimming
it now vs. waiting for long-term treatment — including days-to-long-term and which lots to sell
first.

For the Boglehead approach (mostly buy-and-hold index funds) I realize the "what to trim" angle
matters less. But for the individual-stock sleeve a lot of people still hold, the
**capital-gains timing** part seems broadly useful — e.g. avoiding short-term rates by accident,
and harvesting losses against gains.

Is tax-lot timing something you actively manage, or do you find existing tools (or your brokerage)
already handle it well enough? Genuinely trying to learn whether this is worth finishing.
